# Phase 19 — Forecasting Extension

[Roadmap index and rules](ROADMAP.md) · [Agent router](../../AGENTS.md) · [Analytics architecture](../architecture/06-data-and-analytics.md)

## Goal and Scope

Help users anticipate expected sales, stock depletion risk, and an estimated order placement date. Every forecast has a generation date, horizon, data source, assumptions, and an error estimate validated against history. This extends analytics rather than automatically creating purchase orders or replacing existing critical stock and alerting rules.

This phase starts after completion of [Phase 17](17-observability.md) and [Phase 18](18-production-hardening.md). The first implementation belongs to Inventory Analytics within the analytics service: it receives Sales Analytics and Supplier Analytics data through explicit read contracts without accessing their internal models. Do not introduce a separate forecasting service, Python runtime, or new dependencies without measurement results and an architectural decision.

## Source Data and Semantics

- **Sales series:** daily sum of quantity from fact_order_items by workspace, product, and warehouse on a calendar grid. Account for order status from fact_orders and exclude canceled/returned items according to current business semantics. A day without sales with confirmed import completeness is zero; a day with incomplete data is missing. The data boundary (as_of) is defined by the last _complete_ sales and inventory snapshot date, not the server's current clock.
- **Demand censoring:** sales on a stockout day (quantity_available ≤ 0) may be below actual demand. Do not treat these days as zero demand or use them as regular observations in training and evaluation. If there are too many such days or too few snapshots to check availability, return a limited-quality status. Distinguish forecasts of observed sales from demand forecasts; the latter term is only valid with a verified stockout handling method.
- **Inventory and deliveries:** use the last confirmed fact_inventory_daily snapshot in the same workspace. quantity_available, safety_stock, and reorder_point retain their current domain semantics. fact_supplier_deliveries provides historical lead time; do not treat expected dates in this journal as guaranteed future receipts. The first depletion scenario is calculated **without future receipts** and labeled accordingly in the API/UI.
- **Freshness and suitability:** determine snapshot age, series completeness, the proportion of stockout days, and minimum history before calculation. Demo data ends in 2025: a forecast for a 2026 date must not be presented as current. For demonstration, use a historical as_of and backtest, or update the dataset with freshness validation. For an empty, short, or stale series, show the reason for unavailability instead of a forecast.

## Forecasting and Quality Validation

1. **Build the as-of dataset.** For each product–warehouse pair, store observation intervals, excluded days, input dataset versions (sales, inventory, suppliers), and the cutoff time. Do not use an order, inventory snapshot, delivery, or feature that was not yet known at the historical cutoff. Handle empty days, missing data, and stockout days separately.
2. **Generate forecasts for 7, 14, and 28 days.** Select the simplest valid baseline for the series; clamp negative values to zero. Return an explicit quality status for new products, irregular series, and insufficient history. Category/warehouse totals must be consistent with the chosen hierarchy: either the sum of published product forecasts or a separately labeled aggregate; do not mix both approaches in one metric.
3. **Show uncertainty.** With sufficient history, estimate future observation intervals from rolling-origin backtest errors separately by forecast step and relevant segment; upper and lower bounds must be nonnegative. State the interval level (for example, 80%) only after verifying actual coverage on held-out snapshots. With too few errors, do not draw a precise "80%" band: retain the point estimate with a warning or hide the forecast according to the quality gate. A prediction interval is not a guarantee of stock availability.
4. **Calculate inventory risk.** Sequentially subtract forecast sales from the latest quantity_available, excluding unapproved deliveries. If stock is ≤ 0, the depletion date is as_of; otherwise, it is the first day stock reaches zero. If available stock is already at or below reorder_point, the threshold date is as_of; otherwise, it is the first day the threshold is crossed. If the threshold is not reached within the horizon, do not invent a date. Estimated order placement date = threshold date minus a substantiated lead time from completed deliveries for the corresponding product/supplier; label a past date "order now". If reliable lead time is unavailable, do not show an order date. Separate scenario-based risk ranges from calibrated sales intervals; do not trigger automatic purchasing.
5. **Validate against history.** Use rolling-origin backtesting with the same 7/14/28 horizons, multiple cutoffs, and a final period not used for model selection. Compare against seasonal naive and the 28-day average: MAE, WAPE when total actuals are nonzero, signed bias, error by horizon and segment (including intermittent sales), and empirical coverage and width for intervals. Do not use MAPE for series containing zeros. Separately evaluate accuracy against actual depletion on dates with complete snapshots; when receipts are unknown, do not declare those cases sales model errors. Before publication, record quality thresholds and the minimum observation count in the backtest report; hide failing segments or mark them as unreliable.

## Contract, Execution, and Interface

1. **OpenAPI first.** Add versioned forecast and quality read endpoints to contracts/openapi/analytics-v1.yaml, check compatibility, and generate the TypeScript client. The response must contain workspace, product, warehouse, as_of, generated_at, horizon, daily measured/forecast points, units, model method/version, an interval with its level or reason for absence, quality metrics, data_freshness, assumptions, and an explicit status (ready, stale, insufficient_data, limited_by_stockouts, failed). Protection uses the existing analytics.view and mandatory server-side workspace checks; decide recalculation permissions separately, not through a public expensive GET.
2. **Background computation.** Laravel Queue computes bounded batches per workspace; results and source dataset versions are stored in PostgreSQL. Repeating a job with the same workspace + product + warehouse + as_of + horizon + model_version + dataset_versions is idempotent. After imports, version changes make old results stale; manual runs and scheduled updates use the same application use case. Do not train on page requests. Redis may be used for queues and locking but is not the source of truth. Limit concurrency, series per batch, and execution time; measure duration, errors, stale proportion, and age of the last successful computation according to Phase 17 rules.
3. **UI.** In the product/warehouse context, show actual history as a solid line, the forecast in a distinct style after a visible as_of boundary, and an uncertainty band only for a validated interval. For a saved forecast, provide a forecast-vs-actual comparison for dates with complete actual data; do not retroactively recalculate past forecasts. Below the chart, briefly explain the method, data date, historical error, and the "without future receipts" assumption. Show the order date as a conditional recommendation, not a promise. Explain stale/insufficient_data/limited_by_stockouts states without displaying untrustworthy numbers. The frontend only formats backend-computed values.

## Exit Criteria

- For a representative product set including zero sales, seasonality, stockouts, short history, and two workspaces, forecasts are reproducible with the same as_of and data versions; cross-access to another workspace's series is impossible.
- The backtest is reproducible and published as a phase artifact: source snapshots, exclusions, baseline, errors by horizon/segment, bias, and interval coverage and width are specified. A forecast that fails the preannounced quality gate is not shown as reliable. No future data leaks into features or lead time.
- UI and OpenAPI distinguish actuals, saved forecasts, forecast-vs-actual comparisons, interval bounds, and inventory scenarios; stale data, missing lead time, or insufficient history display an honest limitation. Depletion and order placement dates have distinct labels and verified formulas.
- Queuing, idempotency, retry/failure handling, post-import invalidation, and observability verified. HTTP remains fast during recalculation; migrations and forecast version storage support reruns and recovery.
- [Integration checkpoint](ROADMAP.md#integration-checkpoints) passed: contract → backend → generated client → frontend, applicable unit/integration/API/UI/E2E tests, linters, static analysis, build, and DDD boundary checks. Documentation and ADRs updated only if implementation actually changes the runtime, service boundary, or storage strategy.
